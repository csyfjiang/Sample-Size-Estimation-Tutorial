App 1 Is the development cohort large enough?

This is the first question, asked before any validation: was the cohort you used to build the model (here the Discovery cohort, N = 648) large enough to fit it reliably, so that the model is not badly overfitted? Enter your cohort size and the app reports what each of Riley's four criteria (B1–B4) actually achieves at that size. In practice the shrinkage criterion (B3) is almost always the one that decides whether the sample is sufficient. The example below plans for 20 candidate predictor parameters.

App 2 Is the validation cohort precise enough? (formula)

Once the model has been built, the next question is whether a new cohort is large enough to measure its performance precisely. For your cohort size, the app reports the width of the 95% confidence interval you would obtain for four measures: the observed/expected ratio, the calibration slope, the c-statistic, and net benefit. A narrower interval means a more precise estimate. This tab uses the fast closed-form formulas, the same approach as the pmvalsampsize software.

Drop a predictions CSV (predicted_prob, true_outcome) to auto-fill φ & c
Riley RD et al. (part 3) BMJ 2023;383:e074821. Targets: c≤0.10, slope≤0.30, O/E≤0.22.

App 3 Is the validation cohort precise enough? (simulation)

This answers the same precision question as App 2, but by simulating many datasets instead of using a formula, which lets you relax its assumptions. You describe the model's linear predictor as a Normal(μ, σ²) distribution, and you can deliberately build in miscalibration through the true slope S: S = 1 means the model is perfectly calibrated, while S below 1 means its predicted risks are too extreme. Running it at S = 1 and again at S = 0.9 gives a realistic precision range.

Drop a predictions CSV to auto-fill μ, σ & γ
Snell KIE et al. J Clin Epidemiol 2021;135:79–89.
Set parameters and click Simulate.

App 4 Can you detect that one model beats another?

The final question is whether a cohort is large enough to show that one model's AUC is genuinely higher than another's, when both are evaluated on the same patients. This is the sample-size version of DeLong's test. The correlation ρ between the two models' predicted scores is the key extra input: the more alike the two models are, the fewer patients you need to tell them apart.

Drop a CSV (model_A_score, model_B_score, true_outcome) to auto-fill θ₁, θ₂, ρ, γ
Jung SH. Pharm Stat 2024;23(4):557–569.
